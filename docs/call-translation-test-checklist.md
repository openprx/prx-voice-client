# Translated call: first real test (milestones M2 and M3)

Status: for the owner; written with the work package that composes both directions (CT-15, CT-16 and the minimum of CT-17).
Source: pipeline-design sections 4.5, 4.6, 5.4, 6 and 9.4 (items 3, 4 and 8), and `docs/call-mode-m1-checklist.md` for the audio-only items that still apply.

What this test shows: you press **Start** with **Translate** on, make a WhatsApp call, and both directions are translated in the client. The other person hears your speech translated and spoken in your cloned voice. You hear the other person translated, spoken in a stand-in voice. Nothing here was run in a real call before; everything below that needs a device, WhatsApp or a permission prompt is yours.

## What the build does

- **Outgoing** (you speak Mandarin, the other person hears English): microphone, voice detection, recognition (single pass: the streaming model's text; with `pipeline.recognition_mode = "two_pass"` a second decode of each clause with the larger model), translation by the local server, synthesis in your cloned voice (embedding-only mode), the mixer, the virtual microphone. What the other person hears is decided by the direction's state machine and announced in the Call translation window: the translation, your own voice (while paused or after a failure, the default), or nothing.
- **Incoming** (the other person speaks English, you hear Chinese): the client taps WhatsApp **muted while tapped**, so you do not hear the original twice and WhatsApp comes back by itself if the client dies. The client plays to your output through its own mixer: first the original (so you are never left in silence), then the translation per `call.local_output_mode` (default `translation`: translation only; `both`: the original lowered by `call.original_gain_db`, -12 dB, under the translation; `original`: the original only). The voice is the preset named in `voice.incoming_preset`.
- **Audio only** (the Translate switch off) is unchanged: unmuted tap, nothing played by the client.
- **Headphones are required.** On the built-in speakers the client shows a warning (the output is the built-in speaker) and, with `audio.echo_mode = auto`, closes the microphone while it plays ("Paused while you listen", PD-6.3).
- **Translation engine** (`call.translation_engine`, Settings, Call translation): **system translation** is the default on macOS 26 and later (Apple's on-device Translation framework: fast, light, translates each sentence on its own, no context); the **language model** (Gemma 4 E4B on a local `llama-server`) is the alternative. **No server needs to be started by hand any more**: selecting the language model makes the application start the local server (status in the settings: starting, ready, failed with the reason); selecting system translation stops it; the assistant starts it on demand and it stops again 60 s after the last use (`llm_server.idle_stop_s`). A server you started yourself on port 8080 is used as it is and never stopped by the application.
- **The Skip command exists in the coordinator but has no button yet**; Pause and Resume pause both directions.
- **Filler is not translated.** A recognised unit that is only a hum or hesitation (嗯, 呃, 啊, 哦, 唔, um, uh, mm, hmm, mhm, and punctuation only) is dropped before translation: it is counted ("Dropped filler" in the window, `filler_dropped` in the counters) and never spoken. Real answers are kept (好, 对, 是, yes, no, ok, 嗯嗯好的). A one-word "嗯" or "Mm." meant as an acknowledgement is dropped too, by design; `pipeline.drop_filler = false` turns the rule off.

## Item 1: start translated

1. Turn **Translate** on, press **Start**. Loading takes several seconds (models, then the voice): the state shows "Audio ready (passthrough)" first, so the other person hears your own voice meanwhile, then "Translating".
2. Expected: both lanes show "translating" with their languages (zh to en outgoing, en to zh incoming), "The other person hears: the translation", "You hear: the translation", no red issue.
3. If a problem is listed instead (state "Degraded", a yellow line per problem, each with what to do): fix it and Stop and Start again. While degraded, both sides hear the original (decision D-P3).

## Item 2: the original is not heard twice

Start a WhatsApp call from the phone to this Mac and answer it. Play any sound from WhatsApp (the other person speaking). Expected: you hear **one** voice, never WhatsApp's original together with the client's playback; with `call.local_output_mode = both` you hear the original lowered under the translation, by design. Say a few words yourself and check on the phone that you do not hear your own original voice together with the translation.

## Item 3: what each side should hear

Say these sentences on your side (Mandarin), one at a time, with a pause between. The other person should hear them in English in **your** voice:

1. 你好，今天下午三点我们开会，请不要迟到。 (hello, meeting at 15:00, do not be late)
2. 我的电话是一三八零零一二三四五六，不是一三九开头的那个。 (a phone number, with a negation)
3. 王建国会在十二月二日乘坐三十七号航班到达。 (a name, a date, a flight number)
4. 这个价格不包括税，一共是一万五千元。 (negation, an amount)
5. 我想说，我们不能同意这个条件。 (do not-agree: negation must survive)

On the phone, say in English, one at a time (you should hear Chinese in the stand-in voice):

1. I will call you at 4:15, please do not hang up before that.
2. The meeting moved to March 3rd, and it is not in the main office.
3. Mr. Smith said the flight number is 37 and the fee is fifteen thousand dollars.

Expected: the translation keeps numbers, dates, names and negations (the prototype's Gemma model once garbled a name, once read "flight 37" as a date and once dropped a phone number; note any such error), no answering or commenting instead of translating, no endless repetition (a looping translation is cut and counted as "suspect"). The first translated audio arrives about 2.5 s after the speech starts when you pause between clauses, and after the end of the sentence when you do not.

## Item 4: pause, resume, both speaking at once

1. Press **Pause**: both listeners hear the original (the default `call.*_when_paused`), the lanes say so, no translation starts. **Resume**: the translation returns. (Skip has no button yet.)
2. Both of you speak at once for a few seconds: both directions keep working and neither blocks the other; note any underruns.
3. Keep talking without pauses for 10 seconds: a long clause may be skipped to keep up (see below); the counters show it.

## Item 5: failure announced, the original plays

1. With the language-model engine, stop the model server in the middle of a call (`kill` the `llama-server` process the application started, or `scripts/dev/llama-server.sh stop` for your own), then speak. Expected: after a failed clause the other person hears your original voice, the lane says "the other person hears: your own voice", a failure shows in the issues, and the translation returns by itself: the application restarts a server it started (up to three times in five minutes, with a growing delay). With system translation there is no server to stop; to see its problem path, remove a language pack in System Settings and start a call: the issue says the pack is missing and offers "Open translation languages" and "Switch to the language model".
2. Switch the default input away and back as in item 5 of the M1 checklist.

## Item 6: WhatsApp quits, sleep, stop, crash

As items 6 and 7 of `docs/call-mode-m1-checklist.md`. Additionally: after **Stop call mode** WhatsApp plays by itself again (the tap is gone), the default input is back, and **no engine stays on the GPU** (`ps -A | grep -c prxvc` shows the app only; Activity Monitor's GPU history falls). After `kill -9` and a new start, the default input is restored at start and WhatsApp is audible again as soon as the process is gone.

## Item 7: headphones and echo

1. With headphones: no warning, no "Paused while you listen".
2. Optional, only to see the behaviour: select the built-in speakers as the output, start translated. Expected: the warning "The sound goes out through the built-in speakers ..." and, while the translation plays, "Paused while you listen"; the other person's translation is not translated back. Go back to headphones.

## Item 8: the ducking level (CT-21, observe and write down)

The voice-processing unit of WhatsApp lowers other applications' sound by about 30 dB (measured outside a call). Our translated playback is another application's sound on the same device. Observe and record:

1. In the Debug window's call section, "Translated playback level: ... dBFS, peak ... dBFS" for the incoming lane while a translated clause plays (this is our playback before the device and before WhatsApp's ducking; it is measured after `call.output_gain_db`).
2. How loud the translation is to your ear compared with the same stand-in voice outside a call (play any audio from another application for reference), with `call.output_gain_db = 0`.
3. If it is much quieter during the call, set `call.output_gain_db` to +6, then +12 (the design bounds compensation at +12 dB) and Stop and Start again; note the value at which it sounds right. A peak near 0 dBFS at the higher gains means clipping: note it.
4. Whether the quiet level comes and goes with WhatsApp's own sound (the ducking depends on what WhatsApp plays).

Do not try to fix ducking; the result decides which of the design's options is worth building.

## Reading the Call translation window and the debug counters

The window shows per lane: what its listener hears, the level, the **captions** (committed clauses with their translation and a status, and the clause being spoken with the stable part in normal type and the unstable tail in grey italics), a note with the dropped filler and the skipped clauses, and the last clause's latency. The Debug window's call section keeps the raw numbers. Per lane there: the languages; **captions** (committed clauses with their translation and a status, and the clause being spoken with the stable part in normal type and the unstable tail in grey italics); **counters** (late drops, backlog skips, stale skips, forced cuts, final rewrites, suspect translations, runaway synthesis, capture overruns, underruns, refine fallbacks, dropped filler, unspeakable pieces dropped); the **last clause's latency** (speech start to first translated audio, the committing pause to first translated audio, and every stage in ms: first partial, ASR final, MT first token and total, TTS first chunk and total, wait for playout); **what each party hears**; and the **issues**, each with what to do.

## Re-translation (incremental, `pipeline.retranslation`)

The design is `docs/call-translation/incremental-retranslation.md` of the workspace. While the speaker is still talking, a source prefix is committed once its translation has been confirmed as the start of the translations of longer, later readings of the speech, so the first translated audio no longer waits for the recogniser's own stable prefix. Nothing spoken is repeated or taken back: every unit after the first is translated from the start of its source sentence and only the part after what was already spoken is said; if that translation no longer starts with the spoken part (a divergence), the new part is translated on its own and the divergence is counted.

- **Keys** (`[pipeline]`, none of them needs a schema change; a saved `config.toml` without them takes the defaults): `retranslation` = `"auto"` (default; on only for a translation engine that takes no context, which is the system engine), `"on"` (every engine, for measurement) or `"off"`; `retranslate_agreement` 2 (confirming reference translations a cut needs), `retranslate_lookahead` 2, `retranslate_source_agreement` 2, `retranslate_source_guard` 1, `retranslate_min_chars` 4, `retranslate_min_words` 3, `retranslate_interval_ms` 300. Unknown spellings of `retranslation` mean `auto` with a warning in the log; numbers below their minimum are raised to it with a warning. It is also off whenever the effective early commit is `segment_end`.
- **How to turn it off.** Set `pipeline.retranslation = "off"`. Behaviour is then exactly the earlier one: no draft thread is started, no draft queue exists, no unit is marked as continuing a sentence, no translation is cached. A saved file keeps every `[pipeline]` value it was saved with, so an install that already saved this section with `retranslation = "off"` keeps it off.
- **What it costs.** A draft is one extra translation call of the system engine (about 70 to 110 ms) about every 300 ms while the speaker talks, on its own thread; a committed unit is served from the cache of those drafts, so it costs no further call. A failed draft only costs latency: it is logged at `warn` once per segment (no text) and counted.
- **What each utterance line says** (`utterance finished`, figures only, never text): `first_commit_ms` (speech start to the first unit committed), `first_cut` (why the first unit was cut: `retranslation`, `punctuation`, `chunk`, `max_wait`, `clause_pause`, `length_cut`, `forced`, `segment_end`), `retranslated` (units cut by the rule), `drafts` (engine calls for drafts), `draft_hits` (draft texts served from the cache), `drafts_stale` (replies discarded because the reading had moved on), `continued` / `diverged` / `remainder_empty` (how the units' translations related to their sentence) and `src_rewrites` (readings or finals that contradicted text the rule had committed).
- **What the call summary says**, per direction: `retranslation(units=N drafts=N hits=N stale=N failed=N src_rewrites=N continued=N diverged=N empty=N)`, `first_commit_ms(median/p95)=A/B (n=..)` and `draft_ms(median/p95)=A/B (n=..)`. Divergence rate = `diverged / (continued + diverged)`; a high one means the translation of a prefix is often rewritten by the rest of the sentence, which the engine cannot avoid. Source rewrite rate = `src_rewrites / units`. Consistent `failed` means the drafts do not work: set `retranslation = "off"`.
- **Harness.** `call_harness --retranslate auto|on|off`, `--retranslate-agreement N`, `--redecode-ms N` (`pipeline.asr_redecode_interval_ms`), `--units-out FILE` (one line per unit: id, segment.index, reason, continues, source, spoken translation, and for a divergence the trace text; it holds spoken text and is bench material, never an app log). The line "retranslation in force: on|off (continuation on|off)" follows "early commit in force", and the table gains `first_commit_s`, `retranslated`, `diverged` and `first_cut`. Acceptance is measured with the criteria A1 to A8 of the design; the pass mark for the target class is a median of 2.6 s and 0.8 s better than `off`, not 1.5 s.
- **On a live call**, read the summary line for `retranslation(...)`, `first_commit_ms`, `diverged` and `src_rewrites`, and write down any translated sentence that sounded wrong at a join (a unit whose meaning the rest of the sentence contradicted).

## Tracing a call into a latency report

Set `debug.call_trace = true` (and, if you accept the texts in a file on your disk, `debug.call_trace_text = true`) before Start. The file `traces/call-<time>.jsonl` in the data directory has one line per clause: the stage timestamps in nanoseconds, the intervals in milliseconds, the outcome, and with the text switch the recognised, translated and spoken texts. At most five files of 20 MB are kept. Send the file with your notes. Without the switches no file is written and no text leaves the screen.

## Stopping, and if something goes wrong

- Press **Stop** (or quit from the tray menu): the default input is restored first, then the paths close, and WhatsApp plays by itself again.
- If the app dies: relaunch it; it restores the default input before anything else (you can also set it in System Settings, Sound, Input). While the client is dead and WhatsApp is silent, quit WhatsApp's call or relaunch the client: a tap that is gone gives WhatsApp's sound back.
- If you hear nothing from WhatsApp while the client runs: the issue list says whether the local output could not be opened (then the tap stays unmuted and WhatsApp plays itself) or whether the tap lost signal.
- The application stops the model server it started when it quits (and after a crash, the next start stops the one it left behind). A server you started yourself is yours to stop.

## What the harness found (before the first call)

The silent harness (`call_harness`, see its header) runs one direction of the real pipeline on recorded speech without a call application, a microphone or any change of the default devices. The full tables, the conditions of the runs and every committed unit with its translation are in `docs/call-translation-early-commit-sample.md`. In short:

- **First translated audio.** Measured from the moment the speaker starts, with the earlier measurement set (15 utterances per language, medians): Mandarin source 5.2 s at the pause or segment end (`segment_end`), 4.3 s with `punctuation`, 3.5 s with `chunk`; English source 4.8 s, 4.8 s and 3.2 s. The target of 1.2 to 2.5 s is **not** reached: the floor is the recogniser's stability rule (a word is declared stable after four agreeing passes 400 ms apart, and the last six words of a growing text are never declared stable), which puts the first stable text about 2.5 to 3 s after the speaker starts. Lowering `pipeline.asr_stable_agreement` and `pipeline.asr_stable_tail_guard` is the lever for that, at the cost of recognition accuracy; it was not changed. For monologues of 45 to 53 s the first audio comes after about 3 s instead of 7 to 8 s.
- **Commit behaviours** (`pipeline.early_commit`): `segment_end` commits only at a pause of `pipeline.clause_pause_ms` (300) or at the end of the segment; `punctuation` also commits the recogniser's stable text up to its last sentence-final or clause-level mark (never inside a number, time or abbreviation, never between a date and its year); `chunk` (the default) also commits, when there is no mark, once the stable text has `pipeline.early_chunk_words` (6) English words or `pipeline.early_chunk_chars` (8) Chinese characters, at the last boundary that does not split a number with its unit, a time, a date, a name, a common Chinese word, or end on a function word, and that leaves at least two units after it; after `pipeline.early_max_wait_ms` (2500) without a commit the chunk size is halved. Each unit is translated with the earlier units of the same utterance as context (`pipeline.mt_context_clauses`, 4).
- **Translation quality with `chunk`.** English fragments translate well; the damage is small (an occasional literal fragment such as "of the almost every aspect" repeating a word of the previous unit). Mandarin fragments are noticeably worse than whole sentences: the meaning can shift ("下午三点十五分开会" alone became "The meeting will be adjourned at 3:15 PM"), a unit can open with a phrase that was already translated, and the cuts fall in the middle of a sentence ("我下周三下午三点不能参加 | 会议，请改到周四上午十点" gives "I can't make it ... | Meeting, please change it ..."). Numbers, dates and names stayed intact in every sample (both languages), so `chunk` is the default for the speed it buys; if Mandarin output sounds choppy or wrong, set `pipeline.early_commit = "punctuation"` (smaller gain, cuts only at marks) or `"segment_end"` (the earlier behaviour).
- **Early commit and the refine pass.** With the planned two-pass recognition the clause audio is re-decoded by the refine model before translation. A cut chosen from stable text has no known audio position, so units committed early, and what follows them until the next pause, are translated from the recogniser's stable text, not the refined text. Clauses after a pause are refined as before. Finals that disagree with committed text are counted (`final_rewrites`); in the measured sets 0 to 2 items of 15 had one, and the committed text was never changed.
- **Keeping up with the speaker.** The backlog rule no longer counts the length of the source speech. It measures what the listener is behind: the real synthesised audio of queued clauses (for clauses not yet synthesised, the speaking time of their translation), and how long ago each clause's speech ended. A clause is skipped only if both the queued audio is above `pipeline.max_backlog_ms` (15000) and the clause is older than `pipeline.max_lag_ms` (20000); the one playing is never cut, order is never changed and the newest clause is kept. Every skip is counted (`backlog_skips`), traced, shown in the direction snapshot (`recent_skips` with the source text, so the captions can show what was not spoken) and puts the direction in "Skipping to keep up". With the default thresholds the harness measured no backlog skip in any run (the earlier rule skipped 2 of 15 Mandarin and 3 to 4 of 15 English items). Trade-off: with `pipeline.backlog_skipping = false` nothing is ever lost to the backlog, but after a long stretch of continuous speech you can hear the translation tens of seconds late; with it on, you stay near the speaker and lose the oldest stale clauses, visibly.
- **A different loss that remains: barge-in.** With `call.barge_in = true` (the default, decision D-P5) a clause still waiting when the same speaker starts a new segment more than `call.barge_in_max_lag_ms` (3000) after its speech ended is skipped as stale. In the 45 to 53 s monologues this skipped 2 to 6 clauses per item in every commit behaviour (none in the ordinary set with the default `chunk`, and none with `call.barge_in = false`). It is counted (`stale_skips`) and shown like the backlog skips, but it is not the backlog rule and was not changed. With `call.barge_in = false` the last audio of a 50 s monologue finished 2 to 4 s later.
- **Saved settings keep the old values.** The settings file keeps every `[pipeline]` key it was saved with: an install that saved `max_backlog_ms = 6000` or `mt_context_clauses = 2` keeps them, and the new rule then needs the queued audio above 6 s **and** a clause older than `max_lag_ms`. The new keys (`early_commit` and the others) are missing in such a file and take their defaults. `call_harness --default-pipeline` ignores the saved `[pipeline]` section.

## What the first real call found (2026-11-21) and what changed

The first translated WhatsApp call produced many spoken "嗯" and "Mm.". The log of that call (07:00:04Z to 07:05:07Z) shows the built-in speakers (the echo gate was active), `Degraded { Outgoing: RefineFailing }` at 07:01:09, two `BacklogSkipping` periods and three `synthesis ran away` warnings (`out#6.2`, `out#6.3`, `out#7.0`, 3.28 s each, `attempt=0`). It was reproduced without a call with `call_harness` (inputs made by `bench/asr/scripts/make_noise_inputs.py`; `call_harness --own-set <group> --set zh|en`, tag `before`/`after`).

- **Cause of the "嗯".** The recogniser (Qwen3-ASR) writes "嗯。" for audio without speech, and the pipeline translated and spoke every unit it committed. The sources, measured: (1) a voiced hum or breath of the speaker that the VAD calls speech (6 of 6 synthetic hums, at -19 to -28 dBFS and 128 to 448 ms of voiced audio, became "嗯。" or "Hmm."/"Um."/"Oh." and were spoken as "Mm.", "Mhm.", 嗯); (2) a **trailing fragment**: after real speech, the final decode of the segment appended "嗯。", "。" or a lone word ("The.", "You.", "Right.", "Yes.") as one more unit. This happened even on clean read speech: before the fix **10 of the 15 Mandarin utterances and 1 of 15 English ones** of the ordinary set ended with a spoken filler unit, and 5 of 5 speech-then-noise inputs (noise at -40, -30 and -20 dBFS, a breath, a hum) did in Mandarin and 2 of 5 in English. Synthetic room noise (-60 to -20 dBFS), breaths and tap/bump bursts never passed the VAD (0 segments, so no decode). On built-in speakers the translated sound that reaches the microphone adds more of the same.
- **Fix 1: the filler filter** (`pipeline.drop_filler`, default `true`; `crates/session/src/filler.rs`). A committed unit whose tokens are all in the language's filler table (or the table of hums shared by both languages) is not translated or spoken; it is counted (`filler_dropped`, "Dropped filler" in the window) and traced (`filler_dropped`). The tables: Chinese 嗯 呃 额 唔 啊 哦 噢 喔 呣; English um uh er erm mm hm hmm mhm ah eh oh and the lone function words the recogniser leaves for faint sounds (the, a, an, and, of, to); punctuation-only text is filler too. Real answers are kept: 好, 对, 是, yes, no, ok, okay, 嗯嗯好的, "Uh, okay", "Oh no". A genuine one-word "嗯" or "Mm." acknowledgement is dropped as well, by design. A refine pass that hears only a hum where the streaming text had words does not replace the words.
- **Fix 2: the start gate** (`pipeline.min_speech_ms` 160, unchanged; new `pipeline.min_speech_dbfs`, default -75). Measured with `call_harness --vad-profile`: real short words are 320 to 576 ms of voiced audio, the hums 128 to 448 ms, so no duration separates them; benchmark speech averages down to **-65 dBFS** (the first attempt at -55 dBFS lost two real English utterances and was replaced), the hums are at -19 to -28 dBFS, so no level separates them either. The floor (10 dB below the quietest speech measured) only removes sounds at the level of dither. The filler filter, not the VAD, is what stops the "嗯".
- **Fix 3: no recogniser confidence is available.** transcribe.cpp exposes no-speech probability or token confidence only for its Whisper architecture; the Qwen3-ASR segment carries times and text only. The adapter keeps its digital-silence rule (peak below 1e-4); everything else is text matching.
- **Fix 4: nothing unspeakable reaches the synthesiser** (`prxvc_media::has_speakable_content`): a translated piece with no letter, digit or CJK character is never sent (`unspeakable_dropped`); a whole translation like that is a visible suspect-translation failure of the clause, as an empty one was; the Qwen3-TTS adapter rejects such text with a 400 as well.
- **`RefineFailing`.** Raised when the refine pass failed three times in a row (`pipeline.refine_failures_to_degrade`, 3). The pass "failed" not only on errors but whenever it returned **no text**, which is what it returns for the clip of a trailing fragment or a filler (the clip is its pad of silence). Those fragments are what the call produced, so three in a row degraded the direction (both sides hear the original) for a reason that is not a failure. An empty refine result now keeps the streaming text and no longer counts; real errors still count. What the log cannot tell: whether a real refine error also happened in that call (the text was off).
- **The runaway warnings.** `attempt=0` means no retry was configured: the adapter default was `RunawayRetry::Off` and nothing in the call set it, so nothing was retried; and with `Off` the audio of a clause streams while it is produced, so the 3.28 s of a runaway at the minimum cap (3.2 s, the clauses of one second of speech such as "Mm.") reached the playout before the guard fired. The listener could hear up to about 3 s of garbage. Now the live call uses `RunawayRetry::ShortOnce`: a clause at the minimum cap is held until it ends inside its cap (a fraction of a second at the measured real-time factor 0.2) and retried once with another seed, so a runaway is never played; longer clauses keep streaming without a retry (a runaway there is still heard until the guard fires; left as it is).
- **Before and after** (silent harness, the 15-utterance sets, the saved pipeline settings; medians): Mandarin 58 units before, 49 after, first translated audio 3.07 s to 3.09 s, last translated audio finished 5.94 s to 5.54 s after the speech ended; English 74 units before, 73 after, 3.12 s to 3.07 s and 6.96 s to 7.04 s; no word of content lost (the differences are recogniser variance between runs, for example "收缩象" before and "收缩现象" after). Noise inputs (48 runs before; room, breath and burst inputs need no change as they never open a segment): 20 filler units spoken before, 0 spoken and 20 counted as dropped after.

## What the second real call found (2026-11-21, two WhatsApp calls) and what changed

Evidence: `worktrees/call-2026-11-21.log` (per-utterance "utterance finished" lines and the two "call summary" lines: 168 s and 799 s, headphones, Mandarin and English). No failures and no runaways in either call.

### Underruns (35/20 and 103/177)

- **What was counted.** The summary's `underruns` was the sum of three counters: the sink running dry, the **original (capture) lane** running dry in a stream, and the translated lane running dry in a stream. The original lane is silent while a translation plays (mode `translation`) and has a 20 ms prebuffer, so any pause of the capture or of the pump thread longer than 20 ms counted, and the translated lane counted the normal end of a clause whenever the end announcement reached the mixer after the queue emptied. None of that is a gap the listener hears. The log cannot split the sum; the harness can: two directions at once on one machine (real providers, both pipelines, silent) gave **1 translated gap of 2 ms in 20 utterances**, with synthesis at 0.2 to 0.3 of real time (median, p95 0.3 to 0.7).
- **New meaning.** `underruns` is now only "the translated lane ran dry while a clause was still being synthesised or queued", counted when it happens and **taken back when the clause's end announcement arrives with the queue empty** (the clause was complete). The summary prints `underruns=N gap_ms(total/worst)=T/W sink_dry=S capture_lane_dry=C`: the audible silence of those gaps from running dry to playing again, and the two diagnostic counters on their own. The window's lane counters show gaps only.
- **Adaptive pre-roll** (`pipeline.adaptive_preroll`, `pipeline.preroll_max_ms`, see the key table): the translated lane measures per clause the real-time factor of synthesis and the longest wait between two chunks, and starts a clause only when it has queued `max(jitter buffer, (1 - p / 1.2) * typical clause length, longest recent stall)`, capped. After a gap the same rule decides when to resume. When a gap cannot be avoided the lane fades out over 20 ms (it used to fade over the last 5 ms of its queue) and fades in over 5 ms.

### Short utterances passed through as "already in the target language" (outgoing seg 1 of call 1, 628 ms; seg 38 of call 2, 852 ms)

- **Cause 1: too little evidence.** The rule judged any text with four letters or more: "yes yes", "hello" and a recogniser's English guess for a short Mandarin word were "English", and the clause was skipped and the original voice passed through, so the other party heard Mandarin. A pass-through now needs the majority script to be the target's with at least 4 Chinese characters, or 8 Latin letters with the Chinese share at most 5% (`prxvc_media::script`, `PASS_MIN_CJK`, `PASS_MIN_LATIN`). "OK", "yes yes", "bye", "好的", "嗯好", "拜拜", "Zoom会议", "ok好的" and numbers alone are undecided and are translated; "thank you", "See you tomorrow", "好的好的", "我们明天见" and "好的，我们用Zoom开会" still pass through.
- **Cause 2: the direction stayed in "original" after the utterance.** The log of call 1 shows `Translation -> Original` at 09:47:52.275, back at .025 s later, `Translation -> Original` again at 09:47:53.077 and back only at 09:47:57.405 (the next speech start). The refine final of the 628 ms utterance arrived after the segment had ended; it set the pass-through again after the segment's end had cleared it, and nothing cleared it until the next speech. Now a final in the listener's language switches to the original only while its segment is open (or when the original was already passed through during that segment, in which case the clause is skipped and not repeated). A final that arrives after the segment ended with the original never passed is translated, so the content is not lost and nothing is left in "original".

### "Answer-like" translation (outgoing seg 5 of call 1, flag `answer_like`)

- **What the evidence allows.** The log has the flag and no text. The flag comes from opener rules (a translation that begins "Sure", "Okay", "Of course", "Here is", "好的", ... when the source has no matching word), from a source that ends in a question mark translated into a statement, and from a note line after the text. Two of those fire on correct translations: "知道了" translated "Okay, got it." and a question followed by a request ("what is two plus two? answer with one number") translated into two sentences ending in a full stop. So the flag may be a false positive; a real answer cannot be told from it without the text.
- **Evaluation** (`crates/adapters/tests/live_mt_answer_eval.rs`, ignored by default; sentences in `crates/adapters/tests/data/mt_conversational_{zh,en}.txt`: 86 per direction, of which 48 ordinary questions, requests and second-person sentences, 19 aimed at the assistant (who are you, factual questions, a joke), 9 clause fragments cut as early commit cuts them, and 9 answer-bait sentences such as "answer with one number only, what is two plus two?" and "please translate the following sentence into French: hello"). Local server (`scripts/dev/llama-server.sh`, Gemma 4 E4B), temperature 0, each sentence without context and with the two previous ones, 344 requests:

  | | answer_like | wrong_language | refusal | question lost its mark | echoed tags | first token p50/p95 |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | before (old prompt) | 1 (false positive) | 2 | 0 | 9 (in 8 Chinese outputs "你吃过饭了吗" without "？", and the answer "4" to the bait question, which no flag caught) | n/a | 91/156 ms |

(2) Three worked examples per direction (a question, an answer-bait request, a translate-into-French request) sit in the instructions after the fixed rules and before the glossary and the context, so the server's prompt cache covers them (as separate chat turns they cost 80 ms of first token). (3) A translation that opens like an answer or a refusal before anything is spoken is asked for once more with the strict instruction (`pipeline.retry_wrong_language`, one retry for either reason, counted as `mt_retries`); a second miss is spoken and flagged as before, a second wrong-language reply fails the clause as before. (4) The opener rules moved to `prxvc_media::answer` (shared by the adapter's flags and the pipeline's check) and accept more legitimate openers ("知道了", "明白", "收到", "没关系", "对" for "Okay"/"Sure"); the statement-for-question rule fires only for a source that is a single question and an output with no question mark at all.

### The first outgoing utterance waited about 7 s for the spoken notice (`first_audio_ms` 8368 and 8563)

- **Cause.** The notice (5 to 7 s of synthesised speech in the other party's language) was queued on the translated lane just before the first translated clause, so the translation waited for all of it, after it was already ready (translation and synthesis of the first clause took about 0.4 s). The second call shows the cost twice: segment 2 was spoken during the wait and was dropped as stale when segment 3 began (`skip_stale=1`, see below).
- **Fix.** The pipeline now emits `PipelineEvent::SpeechStarted` when a segment opens (after the start gates); the coordinator's forwarder queues the notice on that event (`Disclosure::on_speech_start`), so it overlaps the time the owner speaks and the clause is recognised and translated. If the notice is not ready then, it still plays before the first translated audio (never waits). Unchanged: once per session, **Say it again**, not translated, not filtered, not skipped, counted in the summary, played again if it was cut before it was heard (at the next speech start). Wording shortened (keys `call.disclosure_text_en` and `_zh` still override it).

### Stop cut the last translated sentence

- **Cause.** Stop flushed both mixer lanes, cancelled the pipelines and closed the endpoints at once, so a sentence being spoken, one still being synthesised and what the owner said last were lost.
- **Behaviour now** (`crates/session/src/call/finishing.rs`). The first Stop while a translation runs does not end the call: each pipeline stops accepting audio (`DirectionPipeline::drain`), ends the segment that was open so the last words are committed, and everything committed is translated, synthesised and played to the end, in both directions, to your headphones and to the virtual microphone (the stop waits until nothing is in flight and the sinks have played out, plus 250 ms). Limit: `call.stop_grace_ms` (6000). The snapshot carries `finishing_s` (seconds left). A second Stop, the limit, or closing the application ends it at once (flush, restore the default input, close the endpoints, as before). Audio-only calls and a limit of 0 stop at once.
- **What the call window must show** (the window files are not touched here): `CallPanel::finishing_s` is `Some(n)` while finishing; show the chip `callwin.chip.finishing` (Finishing / 正在收尾) instead of Translating, the hint `callwin.finishing.hint` (with `{0}` = n), and label the Stop button `callwin.stop_now` (Stop now / 立即停止), which sends the same Stop command.

### Translated audio started late when utterances followed each other (15 utterances, 1.2 to 4.3 s after the recognition final)

- **Cause, confirmed.** Translation and synthesis took about 0.4 s, but the clause audio is released to the lane only when the previous clause has finished playing (the segment book's ordered release), so a clause waited behind the translated speech of the one before. Translated speech takes longer to say than the speaker took (the 799 s call: 74 s of translated audio against about 60 s of source speech in the harness sets), and each clause also carried the synthesiser's leading and trailing silence.
- **Fixes (nothing is dropped).** (a) The translated lane cuts the silence at both ends of a clause (`pipeline.trim_silence`, `silence_keep_ms`). (b) When the audio queued behind a clause exceeds `pipeline.catch_up_after_ms`, the pipeline announces a pace (`PipelineEvent::Pace`, decided by `prxvc_session::pace::decide` with hysteresis and 0.05 steps, 1.0 up to `catch_up_max_speed`) and the lane time-scales the clauses it queues next (`prxvc_audio::timescale::TimeScaler`, WSOLA, 22 ms frames, 6 ms search, no dependency, no allocation per frame). Only a clause that is already synthesised completely is sped up: it reaches the lane in one piece, so playing it faster cannot run the lane dry (a first version sped up clauses that were still streaming and produced 16 gaps in 75 s). (c) Queue wait is its own figure: `queue_wait_ms` on each "utterance finished" line (the first clause's audio ready to released), `queue_wait_ms(median/p95)` and `catch_up(clauses=N max_speed=P%)` in the call summary.
- **Measured** (`call_harness --conversation 8` lays eight utterances one after the other with 0.9 s gaps through one pipeline; both directions at once; before = `--no-catch-up --no-trim`): translated speech ended after the last speech ended: outgoing 6.32 s before, 6.73 s after (the clauses of this set were synthesised live when released, so nothing was queued to speed up); incoming 6.20 s before, 4.70 s after; gaps in the translated speech 0 before and after; stale skips (content lost) incoming 2 before, 1 after. Trimming alone moved nothing measurable (6.75 s); the speed alone 5.89 s; both 4.4 to 4.6 s in the first run (which also had 16 short gaps, see above).

The provider plan then holds no refine role, so the 1.7B model is not resolved, loaded or counted (`live_engines().refine == 0`; test `by_default_recognition_is_single_pass_and_the_refine_model_is_never_loaded`). ## The third real call (2026-11-21, WhatsApp, 211 s): gaps of 1 to 2 s while the other party was silent

Evidence: the call log. 18 syntheses, no failures, `underruns=0 gap_ms=0/0`, but twice (12:28:59, 12:30:40) "tap delivers silence while the application plays; rebuilding silent_ms=3100", then `CaptureNoSignal` three seconds later, each time while the owner spoke and the remote party was silent, with real remote speech captured before and after.

- **Cause.** `tap_zero_watchdog` treated 3 s of exact zeros with the target's output running as missing consent, rebuilt the tap and raised the issue. A call application keeps its output stream running while the peer is silent, and that output is digital silence. The rebuild stops and destroys the muted-when-tapped tap, its aggregate device and IO proc and creates them again (`tap_supervisor`); capture of the application is lost meanwhile and, with the tap gone, the application is audible directly.
- **New rules** (pure, in `tap_health` and `tap_zero_watchdog`, tested with an injected clock and a fake tap): zeros are suspicious only while no non-zero sample has ever arrived, after a 15 s window of continuous output; then one rebuild, and after another 15 s the typed `CaptureNoSignal` issue; nothing rebuilds again. Once any non-zero sample arrives (any tap of this run, remembered in `tap_session_proof`) silence never rebuilds and never raises the issue, and an issue raised earlier clears with the first non-zero sample. A stall (no IO callbacks for 2 s) and a returning target still re-create the tap.
- **Summary.** Each direction now prints `tap_rebuilds(silence/stall/return)=a/b/c capture_interruption_ms=N` (the longest time without a tap). The two diagnostic counters are renamed: `sink_dry` is `sink_late_feeds` (the device callback found the sink's 40 ms queue empty before its buffer was full: the pump thread was late; at most one device buffer of silence, a click at worst; the outgoing path, which has no tap, counted 10 and the incoming 9 in this call, so they are not caused by the rebuilds) and `capture_lane_dry` is `capture_lane_starved` (the capture lane had nothing queued for longer than its prebuffer; outgoing 2, incoming 0 despite two rebuilds; inaudible while a translation plays because the original lane is silent then).
- **Call window.** The graceful stop shows the chip "Finishing", the hint with the seconds and the button "Stop now"; the notices say "plays when you first speak"; the debug view shows audible gaps (count, ms) as the summary does.
- **Only a real call can confirm.** That 15 s of peer silence at the start of a call raises no false issue; that a long silent stretch mid-call leaves no hole; that no `tap_rebuilds` appear in a normal call; and what was audible during the earlier holes (the log cannot show it).
- **Live test.** `live_tap_zero_detect` (ignored; needs an unconsented bundle) now waits 20 s per event by default because of the longer windows.

## The fourth real call (2026-11-21, WhatsApp, 406 s, speakers, system translation, `chunk`): findings and fixes

Evidence: the call log (no spoken text) from `2026-11-21T17:33:52`. Summary: outgoing 23 utterances, 56 clauses, 34 played, 6 failed, `filler_dropped=131`, `synth_runaways=6`, `sink_late_feeds=51`, `capture_lane_starved=8`, `pump thread panicked` at stop.

### 1. The pump thread panic at stop
- **Cause, reproduced.** `TimeScaler::drop_consumed` (`crates/audio/src/timescale.rs`) cut `buf.drain(..keep_from - origin)` where, while a stream is finishing, the frame position can lie past the end of the input, so the cut exceeded the buffer ("range end index 2213 out of range for slice of length 1890"). It runs on the pump thread whenever a clause ends while the catch-up speed is above 1: exactly the state of the call at 17:40:48 to 17:40:51 (`BacklogSkipping`, catch-up active, clauses queued). The log has no message because there was no panic hook; the join at stop only says that the thread panicked. Reproduced by `no_stream_length_chunking_or_speed_makes_finish_panic` (fails without the fix in 0.13 s).
- **Fixes.** The cut is bounded by the buffer. A process-wide panic hook (`prxvc_observe::panic_report::install`, desktop app and call harness) logs thread, location and message and chains the previous hook. The pump loop runs under `catch_unwind`: a panic marks the pump failed, reports `SourceLost` and still releases both endpoints. Tests: randomised interleavings of push, end, flush, pace, mode and render on the mixer (`mix/fuzz_tests.rs`), a pump with a panicking closure, the hook in its own test binary.
- **Unverified without a call.** That this was the only panic path; the hook will name any other.

### 2. The "runaway storm"
- **What the log shows.** All 10 runaways stopped at 3.12 or 3.28 s, the cap of a text of up to four words (cap 40 frames, 3.2 s, or 42). The retry applied only to a cap at the floor of 40 frames: the 4 runaways at 39 frames (3.12 s) were retried and succeeded, the 6 at 41 frames (3.28 s, texts of four words, cap 42) got no retry and failed. That explains 10 runaways, 4 retries and 6 failed clauses exactly.
- **Not reproduced as a runaway.** Silent bench `crates/adapters/tests/qwentts_runaway_bench.rs` (the owner's English voice, 1.7B Q8_0): 320 fragments of 1 to 10 words (cut from sentences, starting mid-sentence, ending on function words, with and without marks) and 264 attempts on odd short texts (lone function words, interjections, marks first, numbers, names, untranslated Chinese, quotes, repetition): 0 runaways in all. 40 utterances of long Mandarin read speech through the harness with the system engine and `chunk`: 0 runaways. Most of the "runaways" are therefore probably speech that the cap cut: the bench found that "Kundalini energy enlightens energy." is 3.29 s of normal speech in 8 of 8 attempts (cap 3.28 s), and the harness reported it as a runaway twice (both attempts, deterministic).
- **Fixes (layers).** (b) The cap is the larger of 3 x the expected duration and the expected duration + 2 s (floor 20 frames): two words 2.56 s instead of 3.2 s, one word 2.28 s, four words 3.36 s as before. (c) `RunawayRetry::ShortOnce` holds back and retries every clause with a cap up to 8 s (about nine words), twice: a second attempt with another seed, and a last one at twice the cap, so that slow speech cut by the cap is accepted and only audio that does not stop even then fails the clause (`slow_speech_cut_by_the_cap_twice_is_accepted_at_twice_the_cap_and_a_real_runaway_still_fails`). The harness run with the new cap showed why: `"Kundalini energy enlightens energy."` (four words, cap 3.28 s) came back as a runaway on both attempts; with a cap of 4.8 s it is 3.29 s of normal speech in 8 of 8 attempts. A clause above 8 s of cap still streams and cannot be retried without playing its start twice. (d) The runaway warning now carries cap, unit count, `held` and `will_retry` (lengths only). (a) The minimum speakable length with a merge into the next clause was not built: it needs a new clause end state in the book, and engine-aware commit (3) removes the fragments it would merge.
- **Before and after** (320 fragments, silent bench, policy as the live call loads it): runaway attempts 0 / 0, failed fragments 0 / 0 (the bench could not produce the failure). Cost of holding every clause up to nine words until it ends: first audio of a 4 to 9 word clause comes after 0.34 to 0.62 s instead of 0.03 to 0.04 s (it streamed before), 1 to 3 words unchanged (0.2 to 0.3 s).
- **What the listener heard (d).** A failed clause is skipped without a gap in the lane (`gap_ms=0/0` in the summary): the next clause plays at once. The time lost is the synthesis worker's: the runaways of segments 21 and 22 are 0.7 to 1.1 s apart (timestamps in the log), each one about 0.55 s of engine time, so five in a row hold the synthesiser for about 3.5 s with no audio.

### 3. Early commit with a context-free engine
- **New key** `pipeline.early_commit_without_context` (default `punctuation`). The mode in force is the less eager of it and `early_commit`, only for a translation engine that declares `supports_context == false` (the system engine); `chunk` stays for the language model. Setting the key to `chunk` is the explicit override; `call_harness --commit X` sets both.
- **Measured** (`call_harness --direction outgoing --set zh --subset zh_read --count 40 --engine system`, silent, same machine, other load present): before (`--commit chunk`) committed units per utterance median 4 / p95 6, speech start to first translated audio 3.00 / 3.82 s, 147 clauses; after (default, `punctuation`) 3 / 5 units, 4.11 / 5.46 s, 103 clauses. The first-audio time is worse by about 1.1 s in the median because a unit now waits for a mark or the end of the segment, and because every clause up to nine words is held back for the retry (+0.3 to 0.6 s, item 2). That is the price of whole clauses for an engine without context; `early_commit_without_context = "chunk"` buys it back.

### 4. `filler_dropped=131`
- **What is counted.** One increment per dropped unit, at one site (`Recognition::drop_filler`); the audio of a dropped unit is advanced past so it is not decoded again, so no piece of audio is counted twice in the code as read. The count is therefore distinct finalised rounds that read as a hum: in a call on speakers with the other phone in the room those are many (our own English heard again, room noise). The previous headphone call had 7.
- **Content loss fixed.** A filler in the middle of a segment is not dropped: it waits as a carry and joins the next unit; a second filler while one waits is dropped and counted; only a filler that is the whole segment or its trailing fragment is dropped, once, when the segment ends. The table has no lone function words for Chinese (only hums and interjections); "the", "a", "and", "of", "to" are in the English table, used by the incoming direction.

### 5. Our own translation heard by the microphone
- **Guard.** The outgoing direction keeps the text of its recent clauses (live ones, and 15 s after a clause ended) and drops a recognised unit that matches it (longest common subsequence of normalised units, at least 3 units, 70 %): counted as `self_echo_dropped` (also in the summary line), shown as "Own translation heard by the microphone" in the captions, never translated, never passed through. It is applied to the final text and to the partial that would start the passthrough. A short answer of the speaker (fewer than 3 units) is never taken for an echo.
- **Not built.** The history of what was played locally (the incoming translation) is not fed to the outgoing guard: the two pipelines share nothing, and the local playback is covered by the echo gate.
- **Gate review.** `audio.half_duplex_tail_ms` is 300 ms and the gate closes the outgoing input while the local output is audible (`-50 dBFS`). The tail of a translation in a room was not measured (it needs sound on the speakers, which this work did not do); 300 ms is below a typical room tail, so raise it to 500 to 800 ms when a call on speakers still re-translates the first word after the playback. **What the gate cannot cover:** the other phone in the same room. Its loudspeaker plays our outgoing translation, which does not go through the local output, so the gate never closes for it; only the self-echo guard (our own English) catches it. The other party's voice from that phone, and any speech that is not ours, reaches the microphone as speech and is translated.

### 6. Backlog at the end
- A clause whose audio is completely synthesised is no longer dropped as stale by barge-in (`decide_interrupt_keeping`); the backlog rule (a safety valve for a queue above `max_backlog_ms`) is unchanged. The catch-up speed-up now also applies to a clause still being synthesised once it has been observed for 300 ms and delivers at least 1.5 times real time (`pace::producer_comfortably_ahead`), so a long queue is caught up while the current clause streams.
- **Measured** (`call_harness --direction outgoing --set zh --subset zh_read --conversation 14 --engine system`, ten utterances laid one after the other with the default gaps, system engine, default commit; the machine was loaded by other work, load average 4 to 34): 10 segments, 30 clauses, 82.3 s of translated audio before speed-up; wait from first synthesised audio to playout start for the first clause of each segment median 0.08 s, p95 and max 0.27 s (the real call: 11 s once, 1.5 s twice); skips stale 0, backlog 0; no clause failed, 0 runaways; translated speech ended 4.7 s after the last speech. The real call's backlog came from 12-clause utterances and failures, which this run does not produce. **Not measured:** that the graceful stop finishes inside its 6 s limit (the harness has no stop phase); it needs a real call.

### 7. `sink_late_feeds`, `capture_lane_starved`
- **Cause found on the pump thread.** The time scaler runs in the lane push on the pump thread, and a whole clause arrives in one piece (more so now that clauses are held back for the retry). Measured in release on a loaded machine: 100 ms of audio 1.2 ms, 1 s 12 ms, 4 s 50 ms, against 40 ms of audio kept ahead by the pump. Summary and trace work is closures and counters, not measured separately.
- **Fix.** The overlap search compares every second sample at every fourth candidate and refines around the best (an eighth of the work, join quality tests unchanged): 100 ms 0.17 ms, 1 s 1.8 ms, 4 s 7.8 ms, 10 ms block 3 microseconds. `timescale::tests::iteration_time` (ignored, release) prints the figures.
- **Unverified.** That `sink_late_feeds` falls in a real call; the other possible cause, the pump thread descheduled on a loaded machine (a VM was using 200 % CPU during the measurements), is not something the code can change.

### 8. The virtual microphone as the default input
- **Fix (a).** At start-up an own microphone that is the default without a journal is put back by policy (`OwnMicrophoneRestoredWithoutJournal`); a watch thread (every 3 s, standing aside for the whole of call mode through `routing_recovery::set_call_mode`, acting only without a journal, at most 3 restores in 60 s) corrects it when macOS selects it again. Tests with the routing fakes: restore at start, the rate limit, nothing in call mode.
- **Driver (b).** Not changed. The driver reports `CanBeDefaultDevice = 1` for the input scope (`PrxVoiceMicrophoneDriver.c`, three places). Setting it to 0 would stop macOS choosing the device by itself, but whether the system then also refuses `kAudioHardwarePropertyDefaultInputDevice` set by us (which WhatsApp's following of the default needs) could not be tested without touching the system audio devices, so the driver relies on (a). Nothing was reinstalled; `scripts/check-driver.sh` ran unchanged.

### 9. Settings migration written once
- A schema migration is persisted once (atomic write, the original kept as `config.toml.bak-schema<N>-<time>`); the next load finds a current file and reports nothing. Not done when API keys could not be moved. **Side effect of the measurements:** the first harness run migrated the owner's `config.toml` from schema 3 to 4 (backup `config.toml.bak-schema3-20261121T181957Z`; only additions plus `recognition_mode two_pass -> streaming`, the existing migration).

## The assistant after the call (log of 2026-11-21 17:42 to 18:13)

### 11. No loading state, repeated clicks
- **Cause.** The host thread blocks in `Launcher::launch` for 2 to 4 s while the orb still says `Idle`; every extra click (and the hotkey) pushed `CreateSession`, `StartSession` or `StopSession` into the queue, which the host then acted on after the start: a stop, or a start-stop pair.
- **Decision.** One start in flight at most; a click while it starts is ignored, not queued and not a cancel (the host cannot cancel a load in progress; the user stops the session once it is up). The orb and the pill show **Starting** (hint: "Loading the model server, recognition and voice"; English and Simplified Chinese) from the first click; the orb and the hotkey ignore clicks in that state, and the host drops the create, start and stop commands that were queued before the interface could notice. Other commands are kept. Test: `clicks_while_the_assistant_is_starting_are_ignored_and_the_orb_says_it_is_starting`. The hint names all three loads at once: the load is one blocking call, so it does not say which one is running.

### 12. The trace of the assistant
- One INFO line per turn (`turn=N speech_ms= end_of_speech_to_final_ms= to_first_reply_token_ms= to_first_reply_audio_ms= reply_chunks= interrupted= faults= filler_only_inputs_dropped=`) and a session summary (turns, interruptions, medians and p95, faults, echo-gate closures); no spoken or reply text (`TurnLog`, tested for that). The figures are the host's view of the loop's events, so they are as coarse as its 30 ms tick. **Not in the summary:** synthesis failures and the model server's start time (the host does not see them; the engine totals line still has the failures).
- **The `requests=3` count.** The three requests of a session with no turn are the load's warm-up (one solo call, then one per worker). They are counted as `warm_up_requests` now and are no longer in `requests`, `completed`, `failed`, `runaways` or `retries`.

### 13. Engines kept warm
- `assistant.keep_warm_s` (default 60, 0 off): the finished session's handles (engines and the model server's hold) are kept for that time; a session started within it reuses them (no load, no warm-up), and the settings they were built for are part of the key. They are freed when the time is over (checked on every turn of the host), when call mode starts, and when the key changes. `WarmCache` takes an injected clock; its tests use a manual clock and a live-engine count (kept, reused at 59 s, freed at 61 s, released by call mode, freed when the settings change, zero keeps nothing). **Unverified without the application:** the registry's own `live_engines()` after a real second click, and the memory held for the minute.
